# Development TODO — Cycle 23: SHARED track · Phase M — the archive meets its own inhabitants

**Planned from the documentation** (the standing rule: the developer
plans when a queue exhausts). Cycle 22 set out to settle the descriptor
space and did — but its sharpest result was unplanned, and it arrived
four separate times without anyone looking for it:

> **Every instrument measures a different population from the one the
> archive is actually filled with.**

L1 found the elites that hold niches forever are SLIP-illegal
**bootstrap** creatures. L2 found the dominant founder lines are
**bootstrap**-founded, not seed-founded, even though every elite was
bred by the adopted layer. L3 found the geometry is calibrated on
`build_population` while the archive is filled by `generate_candidate`
— and the one attempt to measure that stream produced 120 of 600
requested, a bound only. L4 judged a candidate axis on the derivation
population because that is what every instrument uses.

Four instruments, four different questions, one convergent answer.
Phase M turns the instruments around to face the creatures that are
actually in the archive. The L2 lineage seam already exposes the elite
set, so the expensive part is already built.

The identity constraint holds: this is ENGINE depth, not breadth —
with **one deliberate GAME-track slice (M5)** after an archive-heavy
Phase L, because the survey's largest documented gap is player-facing.
Stage 2's undispositioned arms and the unbuilt method tracks stay
named in the standing note and NOT tasked; community/registry stay
owner decisions (DR 0035).
